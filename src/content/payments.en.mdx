# Payment and e-money institution authorisation

<p className="lede">Firms that provide payment services or issue electronic money in Denmark must be authorised by the Danish FSA (Finanstilsynet) under the Danish Payments Act. The rules implement the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the E-Money Directive, which are being replaced by PSD3 and a new Payment Services Regulation (PSR).</p>

<p className="lede">An application for a payment institution or e-money institution licence is extensive. The content requirements are specified in the EBA guidelines on authorisation. Below we go through which authorisation a firm needs, what the application must contain, and what applies once authorisation is granted.</p>

<Toc title="Contents" items={toc} />

<h2 id="who-needs-authorisation">Who needs authorisation?</h2>

Firms that provide one or more of these payment services on a professional basis:

- cash deposits to and withdrawals from a payment account
- execution of payment transactions, such as credit transfers, card payments and direct debits
- issuing of payment instruments and acquiring of payment transactions
- money remittance
- payment initiation services
- account information services

Firms that issue electronic money, such as prepaid cards or digital wallets that hold a balance, need authorisation as an e-money institution.

The application goes to the Danish FSA. A full authorisation applies across the EU, and services can be provided in other member states through passporting via the Danish FSA, directly, through a branch or through agents.

<h2 id="which-authorisation">Which authorisation: full, limited or registration</h2>

- **Payment institution with full authorisation:** for firms that want to provide payment services without a volume limit and across the EU.
- **Limited authorisation:** for smaller firms whose average monthly payment volume does not exceed EUR 3 million. The requirements are lighter, but the authorisation applies in Denmark only.
- **E-money institution:** for firms that issue electronic money. An e-money institution may also provide payment services. A limited authorisation also exists for smaller issuers.
- **Registration as an account information service provider:** for firms that only provide account information services. They must be registered with the Danish FSA and hold professional indemnity insurance.

The right choice depends on the business model, the expected volumes and the plans for other EU countries. A wrong choice costs time, because a new application has to be prepared once the business outgrows the authorisation.

<h2 id="what-the-application-must-contain">What the application must contain</h2>

<h3 id="business-plan-and-budget">Business plan and three-year budget</h3>

A description of the payment services the firm will provide, its target customers, its distribution and any agents and outsourcing, and a budget for the first three financial years showing that the firm can be run soundly.

<h3 id="capital-requirements">Initial capital and own funds</h3>

The initial capital depends on the services: EUR 20,000 for money remittance, EUR 50,000 for payment initiation and EUR 125,000 for the other payment services. An e-money institution needs EUR 350,000. After that, own funds must be calculated on an ongoing basis by one of the prescribed methods.

<h3 id="safeguarding">Safeguarding of client funds</h3>

Client funds must be safeguarded, either by keeping them segregated from the firm's own funds in a separate account or by an insurance policy or guarantee. The application must describe how this works in practice and how the funds are reconciled.

<h3 id="governance-and-internal-controls">Governance and internal controls</h3>

An organisational chart, the allocation of responsibilities, procedures for risk management, compliance and internal control, and a description of outsourced functions and agents.

<h3 id="ict-security-and-incidents">ICT security and incidents</h3>

A security policy with a risk assessment of the payment services, procedures for handling security incidents and complaints, protection of sensitive payment data and a business continuity plan. Payment institutions and e-money institutions are within the scope of DORA.

<h3 id="anti-money-laundering">Anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing</h3>

A risk assessment and policies, procedures and controls under the Danish AML Act, including customer due diligence, transaction monitoring and reporting to the Danish FIU.

<h3 id="management-and-owners">Management and owners</h3>

Information on the fitness and propriety of the management and on shareholders with qualifying holdings, so that the Danish FSA can assess whether they meet the requirements.

<h2 id="psd3-and-psr">PSD3 and PSR: what changes</h2>

The EU has agreed to replace PSD2 and the E-Money Directive with a new Payment Services Directive (PSD3) and a new Payment Services Regulation (PSR). The main changes:

- e-money institutions become part of the rules for payment institutions
- the rules on authorisation, capital and safeguarding are tightened
- the rules on rights and obligations move to a regulation that applies directly across the EU
- stricter requirements on fraud prevention and strong customer authentication

Under the transitional rules, existing institutions must show the Danish FSA that they meet the new requirements. That calls for a review of the current set-up well in advance.

<h2 id="compliance-after-authorisation">Compliance after authorisation</h2>

Authorisation is the start. A payment institution must on an ongoing basis, among other things:

- meet the capital requirement and safeguard client funds
- comply with information requirements and the rules on charges, execution times and liability
- apply strong customer authentication and report fraud and major incidents
- oversee agents and outsourced functions
- comply with the AML rules and DORA
- report to the Danish FSA

<h2 id="questions-and-answers">Questions and answers</h2>

### How long does it take to get a payment institution licence?

The Danish FSA must decide within three months of the application being complete. In practice it takes longer, because the FSA often requests more information before the application is considered complete.

In practice, a high-quality application at the time it is submitted is what decides how quickly the process goes.

### What is the difference between a payment institution and an e-money institution?

A payment institution provides payment services, such as credit transfers or acquiring card payments. An e-money institution also issues electronic money that the client holds a balance of, and may also provide payment services. The initial capital requirement is higher for an e-money institution.
