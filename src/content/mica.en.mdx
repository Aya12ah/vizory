# MiCA and CASP authorisation

<p className="lede">Since 1 July 2026, anyone providing crypto-asset services in the EU must be authorised under MiCA, Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, or, as an existing financial entity, have notified its intention to start providing crypto-asset services.</p>

<p className="lede">An application for CASP authorisation is extensive. The content requirements are set out in Article 62 of MiCA and specified in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/305, the regulatory technical standards (RTS) for the application. Below we go through what an application must contain, and what a firm must keep on top of once authorisation is granted.</p>

<h2 id="who-needs-casp-authorisation">Who needs CASP authorisation?</h2>

Firms that provide one or more of these crypto-asset services in the EU on a professional basis:

- custody and administration of crypto-assets on behalf of clients
- operation of a trading platform for crypto-assets
- exchange of crypto-assets for funds or other crypto-assets
- execution of orders on behalf of clients
- placing of crypto-assets
- reception and transmission of orders
- advice on crypto-assets
- portfolio management of crypto-assets
- transfer services for crypto-assets on behalf of clients

The application goes to the competent authority in the firm's home member state. In Denmark that is Finanstilsynet, the Danish FSA. The authorisation applies across the EU, and services can be provided in other member states through so-called passporting via the Danish FSA.

<h2 id="authorisation-or-notification">Authorisation or notification</h2>

Credit institutions, investment firms, e-money institutions, fund managers, central securities depositories and market operators can in some cases provide crypto-asset services without a new authorisation. Instead, they notify the FSA under Article 60 of MiCA at least 40 working days before they start. Which services a firm may provide this way depends on its existing authorisation.

A notification is simpler than an application, but it must still show that the firm is ready to provide crypto-asset services. Under Article 60(7) of MiCA it must include, among other things, a programme of operations for the crypto-asset services, a description of internal controls and procedures against money laundering and terrorist financing, a description of ICT systems and security, and procedures for segregating clients' crypto-assets and funds. Depending on the services, it must also include a custody policy, operating rules for a trading platform, a commercial policy for exchange, an execution policy or evidence of advisers' knowledge and competence. Information the FSA already holds from the firm's existing authorisation does not need to be submitted again if it is unchanged.

<h2 id="what-an-application-for-casp-authorisation-must-contain">What an application for CASP authorisation must contain</h2>

<h3 id="general-information">General information (RTS Art. 1)</h3>

The firm's name and any trading names, LEI, legal form, company registration, articles of association, head office and registered office, branches, a contact person, and every domain name and social media account the firm uses. A firm operating a trading platform must also give the platform's address and name.

<h3 id="three-year-programme-of-operations">Three-year programme of operations (RTS Art. 2)</h3>

The programme of operations is the core of the application. It must describe, among other things:

- which crypto-asset services and which types of crypto-assets the firm will deal with
- other activities, regulated and unregulated
- the group structure and how the firm fits the group strategy
- the countries where the firm will provide services, and expected client numbers in each
- types of clients, and how they access the services through websites and apps, in which languages
- marketing: channels, target groups and languages
- human, financial and ICT resources, and where they are located
- the outsourcing policy and a list of providers, including within the group
- a three-year forecast with stress scenarios at entity and group level

<h3 id="prudential-requirements">Prudential requirements (RTS Art. 3)</h3>

Article 67 of MiCA requires prudential safeguards equal to the higher of a fixed minimum and a quarter of the previous year's fixed overheads. The minimum is EUR 50,000, 125,000 or 150,000 depending on the services provided. The requirement can be met by own funds, an insurance policy or a combination.

The application must include the calculation, a three-year projection with assumptions and stress scenarios, audited financial statements for the last three years where available, and evidence of the capital, such as a credit institution's confirmation of a deposit or a copy of the insurance policy.

<h3 id="governance-internal-controls-and-conflicts-of-interest">Governance, internal controls and conflicts of interest (RTS Art. 4)</h3>

- organisation chart, allocation of responsibilities and reporting lines
- personal details and CVs of management
- policies and procedures for MiCA compliance
- record-keeping
- how management reviews internal controls, and how independent the control functions are
- market abuse prevention, where relevant
- external auditor and accounting policies
- conflicts of interest policy, including remuneration conflicts, and how conflicts are recorded and handled

<h3 id="business-continuity-plan">Business continuity plan (RTS Art. 5)</h3>

A description of how the firm keeps its services running through disruption. The plan must be tested periodically and cover situations where an outsourcing provider fails or a key person leaves the firm.

<h3 id="anti-money-laundering-and-counter-terrorist-financing">Anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing (RTS Art. 6)</h3>

A CASP is subject to the anti-money laundering rules in the same way as other financial firms. It must know its clients, monitor transactions and report suspicious activity to the Danish Money Laundering Secretariat, the national financial intelligence unit. For transfers of crypto-assets, information on the originator and beneficiary must travel with the transfer under the Transfer of Funds Regulation, the so-called travel rule. From July 2027, the EU Anti-Money Laundering Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 applies directly. The application must show that the firm has assessed its risks and has controls that fit them. It must include, among other things:

- internal controls, policies and procedures under the AML rules
- an assessment of inherent and residual risk across clients, services, distribution channels and geographies
- customer due diligence, and monitoring and reporting of suspicious transactions
- why the controls are adequate and proportionate, including under the Transfer of Funds Regulation (EU) 2023/1113
- the identity and qualifications of the AML compliance officer
- staff training, including on the risks specific to crypto-assets
- how often the controls are assessed, and by whom

<h3 id="management-body-fitness-and-propriety">Management body: fitness and propriety (RTS Art. 7)</h3>

For each member of the management body, the application must include identity, residences over the past ten years, position, responsibilities and a CV covering the past ten years with relevant experience, first and foremost from regulated financial firms and then from crypto-assets, DLT, IT or cybersecurity. Add references, a criminal record and information on pending proceedings, sanctions and refused licences, interests that could create conflicts, crypto-asset holdings, and whether the person is politically exposed.

The application must also show that each member has enough time for the role, including other directorships. Finally, the firm's own suitability assessment of each member and of the management body as a whole must be attached.

<h3 id="shareholders-with-qualifying-holdings">Shareholders with qualifying holdings (RTS Art. 8)</h3>

A chart of the ownership structure with capital and voting rights, and information on each direct and indirect qualifying shareholder under Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/414. It must also show which management body members the shareholders have nominated.

<h3 id="ict-systems-and-security">ICT systems and security (RTS Art. 9)</h3>

Technical documentation of ICT systems, DLT infrastructure and security, including the ICT risk management framework under DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, and the GDPR. Critical ICT services from third parties must be described with contracts, providers and locations. Any security audits, penetration tests or smart contract reviews must be attached. The documentation must come with a plain-language summary.

<h3 id="segregation-and-safekeeping-of-client-assets">Segregation and safekeeping of client assets (RTS Art. 10)</h3>

How clients' crypto-assets and funds are kept separate from the firm's own, how cryptographic keys are approved and protected, for example with multi-signature, and how each client's holding can be identified in omnibus wallets.

If the firm holds client funds, the application must also document that the firm has a segregated client account with a credit institution. The firm must also describe how it ensures that client funds are never mixed with the firm's own funds in that account.

Client funds must be placed with a credit institution by the end of the following business day.

<h3 id="complaints-handling">Complaints handling (RTS Art. 11)</h3>

Clients must be able to complain free of charge and know how to do it. The application must describe who is responsible for handling complaints and their qualifications, how quickly complaints are handled, and how clients are told about the outcome and their other options for redress.

<h3 id="service-specific-requirements">Service-specific requirements (RTS Art. 12-17)</h3>

Depending on the services, the application must also include:

- **Custody and administration:** custody policy, standard client agreement, control of operational and ICT risks, and how crypto-assets are returned on request (Art. 12)
- **Trading platform:** rules for admitting crypto-assets to trading, order execution, fees, price publication, settlement and market abuse surveillance (Art. 13)
- **Exchange:** commercial policy and pricing method (Art. 14)
- **Execution of orders:** execution policy and best execution (Art. 15)
- **Advice and portfolio management:** staff knowledge and competence, and suitability assessments (Art. 16)
- **Transfer services:** management of operational, cyber and ICT risks, and any insurance (Art. 17)

<h2 id="mica-compliance-after-authorisation">MiCA compliance after authorisation</h2>

Authorisation is the start. MiCA sets ongoing requirements for a CASP, which among other things:

- acts honestly, fairly and professionally and gives clients clear information on risks
- meets the prudential requirement at all times
- keeps clients' crypto-assets and funds segregated and safe
- handles conflicts of interest and complaints by set procedures
- manages outsourcing so that responsibility stays with the firm
- keeps records of services, orders and transactions
- complies with MiCA, its delegated and implementing acts, DORA and the AML rules, including the travel rule for transfers

<h2 id="questions-and-answers">Questions and answers</h2>

### Who needs CASP authorisation?

Firms that provide crypto-asset services in the EU on a professional basis, such as custody, operating a trading platform, exchange, execution of orders, advice or transfers.

### How long does CASP authorisation take?

The FSA has up to 25 working days to assess whether the application is complete, and 40 working days after that to decide. The deadline can be extended by up to 20 working days if the FSA requests more information.

For the application process to go quickly and end with a positive outcome, it is essential that the firm's application is of high quality at the time it is submitted.
